# Set the north-star metric and three inputs

Fills section 6. Questions: Batch 5. Method: Amplitude's North Star Framework.

**Plain definition for the owner:** the north-star metric is the one number that shows customers are getting real value. Inputs are the few numbers your daily work can push that make the north star go up.

## Steps

1. **Name the game.** Ask which game the product plays: attention, transaction, or productivity. Amplitude runs this step first because it changes what the north star should be (Amplitude, North Star checklist (undated)).
2. **Find the value moment.** Ask when a customer first gets what they came for. Most products have a handful of key value exchanges (Amplitude, North Star checklist (undated)).
3. **Draft 2 or 3 north-star options** with exact definitions (who counts, what action, what time window). Example shape: "trial accounts with more than 3 users active in week 1" (Amplitude, Good and Bad North Stars (date conflict)).
4. **Run the checklist on each option** (Amplitude, North Star checklist (undated)):
   - Shows customer value?
   - Says something about our strategy?
   - Leads revenue instead of trailing it?
   - Something our work can influence (not the economy)?
   - Plain enough to explain to a non-technical person?
   - Measurable at some point (it is fine if not today)?
   - Not a vanity number?
5. **Recommend one** and let the owner pick. Never recommend revenue, MRR, ARPU, sign-ups, or daily active users: they are lagging or say nothing about value (Amplitude, Good and Bad North Stars (date conflict)).
6. **Pick exactly 3 inputs.** Offer 4 or 5 candidates that work can move directly; owner picks 3. Amplitude says 3 to 5 (Amplitude, North Star checklist (undated)); 3 is this strategy method's house rule. Give each a name and a definition (Amplitude, North Star checklist (undated)).
7. **Link inputs to bets.** Every bet in section 5 should move at least one input. If a bet moves none, either the bet or the inputs are wrong. Ask the owner which.
8. **List what is NOT the north star** (revenue and similar) so nobody steers by it by accident.

## Good to know

- The north star is supposed to be "one level out of reach": you move it through the inputs (Amplitude, Good and Bad North Stars (date conflict)).
- It will change when the strategy changes. Amplitude changed its own at least three times (Amplitude, Good and Bad North Stars (date conflict)). Log each change as a decision note.
- Do not drop a good concept only because today's tools cannot measure it (Amplitude, North Star checklist (undated)). Mark "measure later" and add an instrumentation task to Open questions.
- Product outcomes (what customers do) are easier for a team to move than business outcomes (revenue) (Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees (2023)).
- OKRs are a separate tool. If the owner wants quarterly goals built on this metric, hand off to an owner-designated goals method (unavailable as a Advanced specialist).

## Done when

One named and defined north star, exactly 3 named and defined inputs, each bet linked to an input, and a decision note for the choice.
